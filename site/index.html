The workflow

From paper to formal statement.

MachineChecked brings the original mathematics and Lean declarations together without requiring a new authoring format or a separate verification framework.

01

Open the paper

Start in a Lean project. MachineChecked reads LaTeX or finds numbered statements in a PDF when its typography allows.

02

Connect the Lean

Use existing \lean{...} tags, inspect suggested matches, or choose declarations yourself. No custom claim inventory is needed.

03

Inspect and share

Follow each claim to its Lean statement, inspect the evidence, and export a standalone HTML reader or a hosted audit.

Precise about proof

A link is not a proof. A proof is not a review.

The reader keeps different forms of evidence separate, instead of turning them into one misleading “verified” badge.

Linked to Lean

A repository or reader has associated a claim with a Lean declaration. That association may still need checking.

Checked in Lean

Lean accepts the formal theorem under its recorded dependencies. This says nothing by itself about the paper's wording.

Mathematically reviewed

A reviewer has examined the correspondence, its assumptions and its definitions. The review and its provenance remain inspectable.

MachineChecked performs mechanical checks; it does not certify a paper or endorse the conclusions of an externally published audit. Read more in the evidence policy.

For researchers and formalisation teams

Use it in your Lean project.

Build the Lean modules you want to inspect, then open the paper. The reader runs locally; neither an account nor the website is required.

Terminal
$ lake build
$ uvx --from git+https://github.com/cgarryZA/MachineChecked machinechecked view

Requires Python 3.11+, uv (for uvx), and a Lean toolchain are needed. Use TeX Live for LaTeX, or Poppler for PDF extraction. Alternatively, pip install git+https://github.com/cgarryZA/MachineChecked and run machinechecked view. No Node required; no unexpected Lean build.

Already have a formalisation?

  • Existing Leanblueprint? MachineChecked can use its \lean{...} tags.
  • Paper only available as PDF? It attempts to find numbered statements; locations are approximate and some may be missed.
  • Have formalization.yaml? Its source links and self-declared mappings can be shown with their provenance.
  • Working with a coding agent? Use machinechecked check --json and the agent guide.
  • Want a file to share? machinechecked build writes a self-contained HTML reader.

Portable evidence

Publish an inspectable audit, not a claim of certainty.

Export the reader as one HTML file, or host an audit on your own site. Each audit belongs to its author; readers can follow the evidence and raise issues in the responsible repository. A searchable paper catalogue is planned, not yet available.
